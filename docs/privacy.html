Privacy

NCAA Two-Minute Drill (the website and the app). Last updated October 18, 2026.

What we collect

Nothing we can tie to you. The game keeps your results (which drives you played, your win chances, your streak) in your browser's local storage or on your device. That data never leaves your device and we cannot see it.

Purchases on the website

Cignetti mode, Saban mode and the Tournament unlock together with a single one-time $0.99 payment through Stripe Checkout. Stripe collects your card details, email and billing information; we never see your card number. When you come back from checkout, our server asks Stripe whether that checkout was paid and gives your browser an unlock code. If you use "I already paid" with your receipt email, our server uses that email once to look up your payment with Stripe; it does not store the email. See Stripe's privacy policy.

Purchases in the app

The hard modes are a one-time in-app purchase handled by the App Store or Google Play. To check whether your purchase is active we use RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous app-generated identifier and your purchase receipt from the store. It does not receive your name, email or payment details; those stay with Apple or Google. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

Hosting

The website is served by GitHub Pages, which may log standard web-server data such as IP addresses for security. See GitHub's privacy statement.

No accounts

There are no accounts. The website sends a small anonymous tally to our own server (a random per-browser token, the day, and where the link came from, such as a search ad) so we can count visits and see which campaigns bring players. The daily leaderboard receives your finishing win chance with that same anonymous token. Nothing in it identifies you.

Ad measurement

The website loads the Meta Pixel, Meta's measurement tag, so Meta can count visits, checkouts and unlocks that came from our ads on Facebook and Instagram, and show our ads to people likely to enjoy the game. Meta may use cookies and receive information about your visit, such as pages viewed and your browser and device. If we run ads on Google or X, the website loads their tags for the same purpose. The app never loads any of them. What they collect is governed by Meta's, Google's and X's policies; you can manage how Meta uses this in your Meta ad preferences.
